On the big platforms, the payment rules come ready-made. With direct bookings, they are up to you. A clear policy protects you from last-minute cancellations and gives guests confidence, because they know exactly what to expect. A vague policy gives you arguments.

## The three decisions you have to make

### 1. How much deposit to ask for

The deposit commits both sides. The usual range for holiday rentals is between 20% and 50% of the stay. A low deposit makes the decision easier for the guest but protects you less; a high deposit protects you more but may put off bookings made far in advance. For short stays that are close, some hosts simply ask for the full amount straight away.

### 2. When the rest is paid

There are two common options: the balance is paid a few days before arrival, or on arrival. Collecting beforehand means you do not handle money at check-in and gives you time to react if a payment fails. Whichever you choose, set a concrete date (“up to 7 days before arrival”) and not an intention (“before arrival”).

### 3. What you refund if the guest cancels

This is where most policies fail, because they are ambiguous. A good cancellation rule has tiers with dates:

- Cancellation up to 30 days before arrival: full refund.
- Between 30 and 7 days before: the deposit is not refunded.
- Less than 7 days before: no refund.

The deadlines and percentages are yours to set. What matters is that anyone reading them arrives at the same amount.

## How to communicate the policy

- **Before the guest pays.** The terms should be visible at the moment of booking and expressly accepted.
- **In writing, in the confirmation.** Repeat the dates and amounts in the confirmation email: how much was paid, how much is left and by when.
- **No improvised exceptions.** If you make an exception, record it on the booking. In six months nobody will remember what was agreed over the phone.

## Common mistakes

- Confirming the booking without having received the deposit.
- Not keeping proof that the guest accepted the terms.
- Working out refunds in your head, with different amounts for identical cases.
- Forgetting to collect the balance and only finding out on arrival day.

<p class="nota">This guide is general information and does not replace legal advice. The rules that apply to contracts with consumers may limit your policy; if in doubt, check with a lawyer.</p>
