Almost everyone starts the same way: a spreadsheet with one row per booking. It is simple, it is free and, with one property, it works perfectly well. The trouble creeps in slowly, and you usually notice it on the worst possible day.

## Five signs the spreadsheet is no longer enough

- **Only one person knows how to use it.** If that person falls ill, nobody knows who is arriving tomorrow.
- **You have had, or nearly had, a double booking.** The spreadsheet does not warn you when two bookings cover the same nights.
- **You cannot say off the top of your head who has not paid.** Deposits and balances sit in a column nobody checks every day.
- **The information lives in three places.** The email has the dates, the messages have the arrival time, the spreadsheet has the amount.
- **Doing the year's accounts takes a whole weekend.** Revenue by property, occupancy, expenses: all added up by hand.

## A five-step method

### 1. One calendar per property, and only one

The most important rule: there is a single place where you see what is occupied. Every booking goes in the moment it is accepted, wherever it comes from. If you use Airbnb or Booking.com, enter the booking in your own calendar as soon as you get the notification.

### 2. One record per booking

Each booking should answer five questions without you having to look elsewhere: who is coming, when, to which property, how much it costs and how much has been paid. Add the channel it came from. Later you will want to know where your bookings come from.

### 3. Clear statuses

“Enquiry”, “confirmed”, “in progress”, “completed”, “cancelled”. It sounds like bureaucracy, but it is what lets you answer “is this booking secure?” in two seconds. A booking only becomes confirmed when it meets your rule, for example, deposit received.

### 4. Payments recorded on the booking

Every amount received is logged on the booking, with the date and the payment method. That way the “who owes what” list builds itself and does not depend on anyone's memory.

### 5. Tasks linked to the dates

Every check-out creates a cleaning; every arrival, someone to welcome the guests. If tasks are linked to bookings, changing a date changes the team's work, with no phone calls.

## When to move to software

With one property and few bookings, a well-kept spreadsheet following these five principles may be enough. From two or three properties, or when more than one person is involved, the time you spend maintaining the spreadsheet starts to cost more than proper software.
